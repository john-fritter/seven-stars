# Decisions

The record of what John has decided. DESIGN.md describes how things work; this file says what is settled and why. Anything not listed here is a proposal and can be changed freely.

**How to use this file**

- Add new decisions at the end of their section with the next ID and the date.
- When a decision changes, don't delete it: mark it *Superseded by D-xxx* and add the new one.
- Proposals that need John's call go under **Open** at the bottom, never in the decided sections.

## Framing

- **D-001 (Oct 14)** The project is a death-game reality show on a space station run by an unknown operator, built on a Sims-style life simulation. The death game adds pressure; it doesn't replace the life sim.
- **D-002 (Oct 14)** Setting, in John's words: "a space station built and operated by unknown, and what it is is the set for a game show where the contestants are different species of humanoid that are teleported there with no memories and the whole thing is designed to create drama and tension and horror and comedy. The entire purpose is entertainment value and it never explains itself."
- **D-003 (Oct 14)** An announcer tells contestants what they need to know for the show but never answers questions.
- **D-004 (Oct 14)** Rejected: straight Danganronpa. Its trials are scripted and can't be procedurally generated. Kept from it: murder inside a game that's really about drama, and over-the-top personalities.
- **D-005 (Oct 14)** Rejected: scripted scenes, or a director that decides outcomes. Build conditions for drama and let it emerge.
- **D-006 (Oct 17)** It's a death-game simulator that uses AI, not a society of distinct agents like Mind: the Singularity. One model writes each scene from both characters' information, the situation and the outcome.
- **D-007 (Oct 16)** Dread is the constant backdrop: nobody is there by choice, people have died recently, and who's next is always the question.
- **D-008 (Oct 15)** Drama is procedurally generated by the simulation, with the AI making some guiding decisions and mostly doing the roleplaying.
- **D-009 (Oct 17)** "Agent Terrarium" is a placeholder name to be replaced. Working title: Death Game Simulator.

## Cast, time and death

- **D-010 (Oct 15)** Seven contestants at a time, an odd number for votes.
- **D-011 (Oct 17)** One station day equals one real day in normal running, with fast-forward for testing.
- **D-012 (Oct 15)** Deaths are unpredictable, driven by the meter rather than a scheduled cull. Target roughly two or three a week, to be tuned.
- **D-013 (Oct 15)** Everyone dying is possible; then a whole new batch arrives.
- **D-014 (Oct 14)** Death is always obviously death, dramatic but not too gruesome. Default execution is spacing by robot stagehands.
- **D-015 (Oct 14)** No way out: no space suits, no means of leaving the station.
- **D-016 (Oct 18)** Replacements arrive every morning. Superseded part: trials were briefly set for noon (Oct 16); see D-041.

## The show

- **D-020 (Oct 14)** The core rule is "be entertaining or die."
- **D-021 (Oct 15)** Two separate numbers. The meter keeps you alive. Lifetime points are prestige (leaderboard, hall of fame). Points can't be spent.
- **D-022 (Oct 15)** A vague hint that an absurd lifetime score might let you escape.
- **D-023 (Oct 17)** Hitting zero puts a contestant on notice. Anyone at zero at the evening ceremony is spaced.
- **D-024 (Oct 15)** An LLM judge scores scenes, but only some of the time. Deterministic scoring covers everything else.
- **D-025 (Oct 15)** Viewers decide what's entertaining. The operator is an entertainment machine: it uses viewer feedback when it has some and its own taste when it doesn't.
- **D-026 (Oct 15)** Staged drama follows the rule of cool: if it's great, it's great; if it's lame, it isn't.
- **D-027 (Oct 15)** A producer layer writes "Previously on" recaps built only from what actually happened, with links into the events.
- **D-028 (Oct 18)** The Wheel: once a day the bottom-ranked contestant spins it. It's scary to everyone, most of all the spinner. It can kill or give immunity, make someone miserable or comfortable, affect someone else or everyone, and launch contests. Segments as listed in DESIGN.md (John edited them on Oct 18).

## Rank, rooms and privileges

- **D-030 (Oct 17)** Everything goes by rank, not tiers. The top contestant has the best of everything; the bottom one lives like they're homeless.
- **D-031 (Oct 17)** Rooms: one suite, one good room for two, one basic dorm for three, each with its own bathroom. The dorm's bathroom has cold water only.
- **D-032 (Oct 18)** The bottom contestant sleeps on a couch and doesn't get to shower at all. (They can take sink baths: D-095.)
- **D-033 (Oct 18)** The bottom contestant is locked out of the lounge and the rec room.
- **D-034 (Oct 18)** One eating area, not rank-split canteens, so people mix. Set mealtimes. Robots serve each person a meal type by rank. A cafe serves breakfast and lunch; dinner is in the lounge.
- **D-035 (Oct 18)** Booze is never a punishment; it fuels drama. Everyone gets booze in quality levels by rank: fancy, good, cheap, and rotgut for the bottom. The good stuff is a much more enjoyable experience.
- **D-036 (Oct 18)** Personality affects the pull toward drinking, as it does everything else people can do.
- **D-037 (Oct 18)** Ranks are assigned at the evening ceremony for the next 24 hours, with that day's newcomers ranked alongside everyone. Rank ties break on lifetime points.
- **D-094 (Oct 18)** Every bedroom has its own bathroom with a toilet, sink and shower: hot water in the suite and good room, cold in the dorm. So you can get a better shower by dating up, if the person lets you in.
- **D-095 (Oct 18)** The bottom rank washes with sink baths in the public restroom.
- **D-096 (Oct 18)** Anyone can knock and ask an occupant to let them into a bedroom. If the relationship is good enough the occupant may say yes; asking can also be annoying. Someone let in is a legal guest. (Odds and limits proposed in DESIGN.md.)

## Rules and punishments

- **D-040 (Oct 17)** Rule five is "don't interfere with the cameras," so contestants still believe some places are more private.
- **D-041 (Oct 18)** Daily order: in the morning, replacements arrive and then any pending trials are held, so the newcomer is aboard to break ties. A tie that still happens breaks on the meter, then lifetime points. At the evening ceremony, anyone at zero is spaced, then ranks are assigned.
- **D-042 (Oct 17)** Enforcement happens at the evening ceremony.
- **D-043 (Oct 17)** Punishments: the fishbowl (a glass confinement cell), a meter penalty that drops you down the ranks (instead of demotion), collective punishment, and playing a confessional at dinner. No branding signs.
- **D-044 (Oct 16)** Rejected: the stockade. Wrong vibe.

## Murder and trials

- **D-050 (Oct 15)** The operator rewards murder, but murder should be challenging. "The point is drama, not bloodsport." There shouldn't be multiple murder mysteries going at once.
- **D-051 (Oct 16)** No mechanical lockdown after a death.
- **D-052 (Oct 15)** Killing earns secret points.
- **D-053 (Oct 15)** Secret immunities, such as extra lives, exist.
- **D-054 (Oct 15)** Accusation trials: the accuser lays out the case, the accused defends, contestants vote, and the loser (accuser or accused) is spaced. Contestants vote, not viewers.
- **D-055 (Oct 16)** No investigation mechanics. The group is small; trials run on testimony. The announcer can divulge information, since the whole station is observed.
- **D-056 (Oct 16)** Crimes of passion come from mental breaks, with the break type tied to whatever triggered it. The AI doesn't need to decide to kill.

## Station and look

- **D-060 (Oct 15)** Top-down or isometric view, RimWorld-like.
- **D-061 (Oct 15)** Seventies sci-fi luxury: a cross between 2001: A Space Odyssey, Star Trek, Starbound and Big Brother.
- **D-062 (Oct 15)** No work. There are activities, needs, privileges and things to compete for. Rich but uncomfortable: not enough space or best food, people disturb each other's sleep, modesty, substances, varied recreation, hygiene that lacks privacy.
- **D-063 (Oct 15)** No truly private areas. Viewers see everything; some areas may feel more private to contestants.
- **D-064 (Oct 15)** Robot stagehands serve contestants and carry out executions.
- **D-065 (Oct 18)** *Secret room superseded by D-097.* Layout: a big common room in the middle with couches, where ceremonies and trials happen; a cafe; a lounge with bar, dance floor, karaoke stage and a big window into space; a "secret" room with no cameras (as far as contestants know); a cage room to watch cage matches; a garden. Floated: a spa for the top three ranks; a bowling alley, arcade and rec room serving booze and snacks.
- **D-066 (Oct 18)** Clothing is a feature: procedurally generated outfits with simple effects, showing status and personality. (How it works: D-103.)
- **D-097 (Oct 18)** The "secret room" is a public restroom attached to the common room: sinks, lockable stalls, a wardrobe of paper clothes, no visible cameras. It has no shower.
- **D-098 (Oct 18)** The public restroom is unisex. Rejected: gendered restrooms. A continuous gender axis needs a sorting rule, and the drama would map onto the real-world trans bathroom fight rather than the show's own cruelty.
- **D-099 (Oct 18)** Temperature is in, including in slice one. Bedrooms have their own thermostats; the operator sets the rest, and the fishbowl is slightly too cold. Temperatures always stay in a range that harms no one. Species have slightly different comfort ranges, and clothing and its quality shift them.
- **D-103 (Oct 18)** Clothes are a look, not inventory items. Each contestant has a signature outfit for life (so characters stay distinguishable), embellished by rank tier; rank 7 wears a paper jumpsuit. The show owns the clothes: robots deliver or repossess at the ceremony. Effects stay simple (warmth, a little beauty, a vanity thought). Rejected for now: owning, swapping and stealing clothes.

## Characters and romance

- **D-070 (Oct 15)** *Refined by D-101.* Species are simple, distinguishable, recognizable and cute, in the spirit of Starbound or FTL. One species has a strength bonus, one a penalty.
- **D-071 (Oct 15)** Every contestant has randomized stats: strength, personality, attractiveness and more. Personality works like The Sims' sliders, possibly with traits or quirks on top. A generator builds varied personalities from probability plus LLM help.
- **D-072 (Oct 15)** Minimal health. Mood and mental breaks are in.
- **D-073 (Oct 15)** Beauty is a stat that affects attraction universally, though personality can matter more.
- **D-074 (Oct 15)** Gender is a feminine-to-masculine axis. Orientation is two axes, attraction to femininity and to masculinity, which also stand in for desire.
- **D-075 (Oct 15)** A monogamy axis. Each character has an ideal personality; the closer someone is to it, the likelier a crush.
- **D-076 (Oct 15)** No species preference in attraction.
- **D-077 (Oct 15)** Lovin' is always consensual and shown only by implication (lumps under the covers, censor bars). PG-13, safe for work.
- **D-078 (Oct 15)** No pregnancy or births.
- **D-100 (Oct 18)** Everyone has a bladder need (as in RimWorld's Dubs Bad Hygiene and The Sims). Nothing gross: a toilet visit takes a few minutes, and there are no accidents.
- **D-101 (Oct 18)** Species are sexy aliens, not monster aliens: visually recognizable, not very mechanically unique. No species-only needs (the "wet" need) or species habits like snoring. The placeholder species (Bronte, Pip, Lumen, Naut) are retired. The roster:
  1. **Human:** familiar, varied, ordinary; the baseline.
  2. **Elfin:** taller, slender, elongated ears, unusual eye and skin colors. Beauty bonus, strength penalty.
  3. **Horned:** strongly built, horns, tails, unusual skin, maybe facial markings. Strength bonus, hunger drains faster.
  4. **Feline:** shorter, expressive, large eyes, pointed ears, tails; more humanoid than animal. Doesn't need to shower, fun drains faster.
  5. **Aquatic:** sleek, smooth-skinned, striking eyes, delicate gills, maybe bioluminescent markings.
  6. **Reptilian:** fine scales, angular features, unusual pupils, colorful crests or markings; sophisticated, not crocodile-headed. Prefers heat, sleeps more, less bothered by social debuffs.
- **D-102 (Oct 18)** The aquatic species' difference lives in hygiene: it drains faster and any water refills it, cold included. Felines groom instead of showering. The elfin beauty bonus stays small. Snoring stays as an individual quirk anyone can roll.
- **D-104 (Oct 21)** Every bed is shareable. People want to share a bed with someone they're attracted to and dislike sharing with someone they aren't.
- **D-105 (Oct 21)** The bed is the default place for lovin'. Lovin' outside the bedroom isn't forbidden, but it's taboo: it affects whoever witnesses it, and personality (modesty above all) decides who risks it. Lovin' lasts a variable time, about five to thirty minutes.
- **D-106 (Oct 21)** Modesty drives the desire for privacy, for lovin' and for showering and the like.
- **D-107 (Oct 21)** The occasional night owl: a quirk that shifts the hours they like to be awake. People can disturb each other's sleep.

## Viewers

- **D-080 (Oct 15)** Viewers can upvote or downvote almost anything and leave comments.
- **D-081 (Oct 16)** No direct control: viewers can't save or doom anyone. They're one voice in the audience, maybe the only one, and the judge weighs that feedback heavily.
- **D-082 (Oct 15)** It must work with a single viewer: if John is the only one watching, he is the audience's opinion.
